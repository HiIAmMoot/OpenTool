Bakes need much less memory, Max resolution picks the page size for you, and a finished bake can be written to the tags again without baking.

## Download

`OpenTool-v0.2.2-windows-x64.zip` holds `OpenTool.exe` and `OpenTool-cli.exe`. Unzip it and run `OpenTool.exe`. The only thing to install is [Blender 5.2](https://www.blender.org/download/) or newer.

## New

- Max resolution has an Auto setting, and it's the default. It sizes the pages from the level's biggest surface and the texel density, then packs them full. Small charts go into the gaps of big ones, so lightmaps at Ultra are half the size on Blood Gulch and 17% smaller on Monaco.
- The bake works on small pages and only the saved tags get the big ones. A high resolution no longer needs tens of gigabytes: Timberland at Ultra went from 14.8 to 3.4 GB.
- The glow and vertex steps use less memory (Monaco's vertex step went from 26.5 to 17.2 GB).
- Faster bakes when there's memory to spare. Timberland at Ultra: 1340 s down to 1061 s, or 2257 s down to 1335 s with adaptive sampling off.
- Keep a finished bake and write it again, e.g. as 32-bit or to other tag names.
- Ultra preset.
- Advanced explains each setting and shows an example image where it helps.
- Adaptive sampling & render-only shadows are now in Advanced. Render-only surfaces (like sea planes) shadow through their bump map's alpha; pick "None" for tool's behaviour.
- The memory estimate covers every stage and never holds up Bake. You get a quick figure right away, and it gets more exact while you wait.
- Bake report in the log: time per stage, page and file sizes.

## Fixes

- A surface too big for one page stopped the bake (c40, b40). It's now cut to fit.
- Water and render-only surfaces blocked light.

## Known limits

- Enhanced ignore-normals lighting turned out overbright, so it's experimental and hidden from the app. Ultra uses Traditional. To try it anyway, put `"ignore_normals_mode": "enhanced"` in a `--settings` file.
- The memory figure is an estimate.
